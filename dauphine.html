Study 08

Europe and the Dauphine

The King of France, knowing full well what this symbolism represented, knew it to be the King of the Fish, or as in correspondence a symbol of Christ as King of the Fish. So the King of France decided to have the symbol for themselves, for their bloodline and for their future king or heir apparent.

In 1349, King Charles V was the first King of France to have his shield quartered with a symbol of the future king or heir apparent placed upon it, in the symbol form of the Dolphin.

The Dolphin, the king of the fish, as Christ was frequently called in those days, is known as the helpful animal, par excellence. The word Dolphin is derived from the Celtic language, broken into three syllables: Dal, which means district; Na, which means of the; and Pen or Pin, which means point, hill, head, chief or leader. So a Dolphin would be a very excellent symbol for the leader of any persuasion to have as their emblem.

Heraldry: Sources, Symbols and Meanings. Ottfried Neubecker. Pages 98–103.

The rulers of the province of Dauphiné carried the title Dauphin. The last Dauphine, Anne de Viennois, bequeathed the title to the house of Latour-du-Pin. In 1335, the last male of this house sold the land to the King of France. Since then, the heir apparent to the throne of France has been called Dauphin.

Symbolic & Mythological Animals. J.C. Cooper. Aquarian/Thorsons. Page 80.

The Dolphin is par excellence the helpful animal.

The Secret Teachings of All Ages. Manly P. Hall. Page LXXXV.

The heir to the throne of France, the Dauphin, may have received this title from this ancient pagan symbol of the divine preservative powers.

A Concise Encyclopedia of Heraldry. Guy Cadogan Rothery. Page 57.

The word Dolphin is supposed to have been derived from Celtic Dal = District, Na = of the, and Pen or Pin = Point, hill, head, chief, leader.
